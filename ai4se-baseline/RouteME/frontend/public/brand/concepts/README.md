# RouteMe 路觅 — alternative directions

Four different ideas, all drawn in the same system (rounded badge, teal→azure gradient,
white ink, coral accent) so they compare fairly. Start with `concepts-overview.png`
(marks) and `concepts-lockups.png` (mark + wordmark together).

The wordmark, palette and badge in these files are identical to the primary logo in
`../`; only the mark changes.

---

## A — Two paths, one place · `concept-a-two-paths-*`
A map pin whose tip is fed by two paths, one white and one coral: two people,
coming from different directions, meeting at the same destination. The coral path
tucks behind the marker so the destination stays clean, and both paths narrow as
they arrive, so the shape flows instead of colliding.

**Reads as:** "let's go to the same place" — the match-making part of the product.
**Strength:** most specific to RouteMe's proposition. **Risk:** at very small sizes
the two paths merge into one Y, so the "two people" nuance softens.

## B — R as a route · `concept-b-r-monogram-*`
The letter R drawn with the same rounded, tapered stroke language as a route, the
leg ending on a coral destination dot. No literal travel objects — it works as a
monogram and as a shape.

**Reads as:** a confident brand initial. **Strength:** the most "logo-like" and the
most versatile (works tiny, works as a favicon, works without the wordmark).
**Risk:** needs the name next to it before it says "travel".

## C — Pin + conversation · `concept-c-pin-chat-*`
A location pin whose head holds three coral dots, i.e. a conversation happening at
a place. The pin silhouette doubles as a speech bubble, which is the mashup the
product actually is: maps + community.

**Reads as:** "talk about where you're going". **Strength:** friendliest of the
four, immediately social and travel at once. **Risk:** the dots disappear first when
scaled down to favicon size.

## D — Route across the world · `concept-d-globe-*`
A clean globe (ring + meridian + latitudes) in white, deliberately without an accent
dot — the badge gradient carries the colour instead.

**Reads as:** international travel, reach, "anywhere". **Strength:** the most
instantly understood travel symbol, very calm. **Risk:** the least specific to a
*social* product, and the most detailed of the four at 16–24 px.

---

## If you want a recommendation

- **B** is the safest long-term brand mark: it survives every size and never needs
  an explanation.
- **A** is the best storytelling mark for a pitch or landing page, and pairs well
  with the current primary logo.
- **C** is the best fit for an app icon on a phone home screen.
- **D** is the safest "travel" read for users who have never heard of RouteMe.

Any of them can be pushed further — different accent colour, no badge (mark on
transparent), outlined/line-art version, or an animated route for the splash screen.
